The visibility gap

Strong offline. Inconsistent on the map.

Plenty of well-run local businesses have the things that should matter: years of work behind them, good reviews, repeat customers and a reputation that holds up in the area they serve.

That reputation doesn't always carry over to the map. The same business can appear clearly for someone searching a few streets away and barely appear for someone searching from the other side of the same service area. Most owners never see this, because they only ever search from their own location.

That difference between what a business has earned locally and how it shows up on the map is the gap worth looking at. It can be measured point by point — and that's where we start.

Findings

  1. Finding 01

    Observation: Visibility is strong within roughly two kilometres of the business and drops sharply across the southern and eastern parts of the stated service area.

    What we'd investigate: Profile relevance for those areas, competitor positioning at those search points, and other local signals that may be contributing to the gap.

  2. Finding 02

    Observation: The profile lists a narrower set of categories and services than the business appears to actually offer.

    What we'd investigate: Which categories and services the strongest local competitors use, and whether the current selection matches how customers in this area search.

  3. Finding 03

    Observation: Review volume is healthy overall, but recent reviews are sparse compared with the competitors appearing above this business in the weaker areas.

    What we'd investigate: Review recency and response patterns relative to those competitors, and whether this is worth testing as a contributing factor.

  4. Finding 04

    Observation: Business name, address and phone details differ between the profile, the website and several local directory listings.

    What we'd investigate: Where the inconsistencies originate and which listings are worth correcting first.

Scope

What we look at

  • Google Business Profile — how it's set up and what it communicates.
  • Maps visibility across the service area — measured from multiple points, not just one.
  • Categories and services — what's listed versus what the business actually does.
  • Reviews and reputation signals — volume, recency and how they're handled.
  • Local competitors — who appears where you don't, and how they're positioned.
  • Business information consistency — name, address, phone and hours across the places they appear.
  • Other observable local search gaps — anything measurable that stands out for this specific business.

We report what's observable. Where something needs testing rather than guessing, we say so.
